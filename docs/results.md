# Results, in depth

Every result below is on [`/evidence`](https://giftwise-405i.onrender.com/evidence) and its pages, case by case, with the sources. Back to the [README](../README.md).

## Two Claude reviewers, two rulebooks, and a referee in code

- **When it runs.** A bill that might be a housing cost goes to two Claude reviewers: one reads only the regulations and the Federal Register, the other only SSA's operating manual. A bill uploaded on `/try` goes to them whenever its category sits at or near housing (insurance, a deposit, HOA dues, water, sewer, trash, internet, a utility or rent) or Claude read it with under 90% confidence (`src/lib/review/bill-route.ts`, `src/lib/ai/bill-review.ts`).
- **What each must do.** Quote its rulebook word for word. A program checks every quote.
- **What the referee does.** Giftwise takes a reading only when both checked answers agree; otherwise it uses the route that keeps the check whole under either reading.
- **One recorded bill.** Homeowners insurance her lender requires. The regulations reviewer says it is not a housing cost, the manual reviewer says it is, both quotes check out, and the referee sends the $150 to her ABLE account, where her June 2026 check is $994 either way (paid to the insurer, it could be $924). It is on [`/evidence/rules#referee`](https://giftwise-405i.onrender.com/evidence/rules#referee).
- **Measured on 59 bills** labeled from the rule text (20 plus 39 harder ones: internet, renter's insurance, HOA fees, bundled bills, a utility deposit). The referee and the manual reviewer accepted 0 routes that would lower the check, the regulations reviewer 1, and Claude with no rulebook 3. All 118 quotes were found word for word.
- **Her check.** The referee kept her check whole on 59 of 59 bills (the 52 with a clear answer and the 7 contested): it settled 43 of the 52 clear bills on a shared, checked reading and gave the other 9 the route that keeps the check whole under either reading.
- **Each reviewer alone, from the same run.** The manual reviewer alone kept her check whole on 59 of 59, with no wrong routes, and settled 51 of the 52 clear bills; the regulations reviewer alone kept it whole on 58 of 59, accepting one wrong route (homeowners insurance). On this run, then, the manual reviewer alone was as safe as the referee and settled more bills. The table is on [`/evidence/rules#evaluation`](https://giftwise-405i.onrender.com/evidence/rules#evaluation).
- **What the second reviewer adds.** Which reviewer errs is not known before a bill arrives. When one reviewer is wrong, trusting it alone keeps her check whole on 28 of 59; with both reviewers and the referee, 59 of 59. The cost, measured: 9 clear bills go to the route that keeps the check whole under either reading (her ABLE account) instead of being settled, each with her check whole.
- **The referee under pressure, in code.** A recorded adversarial set (`src/lib/review/adversarial.ts`, held by `tests/referee-adversarial.test.ts`) sends the referee reviewer outputs a model could produce and must not be trusted, built against the real rulebook passages with no model call: a quote that is not in the passage it cites, a quote from the other reviewer's rulebook, the right answer with a citation that does not exist, and two reviewers who disagree with both quotes checked. The referee sets the first three aside with the reason, abstains in all four, and routes the bill the way that keeps her check whole under either reading; the honest pair is accepted. The table is on [`/evidence/rules#referee-adversarial`](https://giftwise-405i.onrender.com/evidence/rules#referee-adversarial).

### What the AI adds, measured

The same 59 recorded bills, with a fourth router beside the others: one with no AI, which never reads the bill and always takes the route that is safe under both readings (`src/lib/review/what-ai-adds.ts`, no model call, held by `tests/what-ai-adds.test.ts`). It is on [`/evidence/rules#what-ai-adds`](https://giftwise-405i.onrender.com/evidence/rules#what-ai-adds).

| All 59 bills | No AI | Regulations alone | Manual alone | Both reviewers, referee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Her check kept whole | 59 of 59 | 58 of 59 | 59 of 59 | 59 of 59 |
| Wrong routes accepted | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| Settled with nothing left for her to do: paid straight to the business, of the 28 that are not housing | 0 of 28 | 22 of 28 | 28 of 28 | 22 of 28 |

With no AI, always taking the route that is safe under both readings keeps her check whole on 59 of 59 bills with 0 wrong routes, the same as the referee: on these bills the AI does not make the check safer. What the AI adds is settling bills: the referee pays 22 of the 28 bills that are not housing straight to the business, with nothing left for her to do, while the router with no AI pays 0 that way and sends 59 to her ABLE account, where she pays each bill herself.

The referee is there for the cases in `src/lib/review/adversarial.ts`, where one reviewer would mislead the router: a quote that is not in its rulebook, a quote from the other rulebook, a made-up citation, and two readings that disagree. On those five cases, a $138 electric bill paid by family in April 2026, computed from the same code:

| The five adversarial cases | Her check kept whole |
|---|---|
| Regulations reviewer alone, its quote checked | 5 of 5 |
| Manual reviewer alone, its quote checked | 4 of 5 |
| Regulations reviewer alone, its answer taken as sent | 4 of 5 |
| Manual reviewer alone, its answer taken as sent | 3 of 5 |
| Both reviewers and the referee | 5 of 5 |

With its quote checked, the regulations reviewer alone is as safe as the referee here; the manual reviewer alone is not, because when the two disagree its quote checks out, its reading pays the company, and her June 2026 check drops from $994 to $876. The quote check catches the wrong quote, rulebook and citation; the second reviewer catches a reading that checks out and is still wrong.

## The two errors that cost money

Counted over every case whose right answer is known (`src/lib/error-rates.ts`, from the recorded runs):

- **0 of 55** gifts that would lower the check were let through as given (SSA's examples 0 of 17, court cases 0 of 14, and 0 of the 24 clear bills that are housing costs). Claude with no rulebook, on the same 24 bills: 1 of 24. The 7 contested bills have no single right answer and count in neither rate, which is why these denominators add to 52 bills, not 59.
- **6 of 51** gifts that were fine as given were sent another way (SSA's examples 0 of 23, and 6 of the 28 clear bills that are not housing costs). All 6 are hard bills the two reviewers did not settle, so the money went to her ABLE account instead of the shop, and her check stayed whole. Claude with no rulebook, on the same bills: 4 of 28.

## Checked against Social Security's own examples

40 worked examples from SSA's operating manual and regulations, each with SSA's answer quoted word for word. The rules engine matches all 40. Claude alone, with no rulebook, matches 32: it misses the $60 one-off exclusion six times and housing support twice. Growing the set from 13 to 40 added two finer points the engine now follows: the $60 exclusion applies in order of receipt and before the housing cap, and a prepaid housing bill counts in the months it covers. `npm run eval:ssa` reruns it.

**Held out.** Because the engine changed while the set grew, the engine was then frozen at commit `3f3ea60` (copied out of git) and run once on worked examples from SSA's manual that had never been scored or used to change it: four gift-card examples from [POMS SI 00830.522](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500830522) and the one-third reduction after a cost-of-living increase from [POMS SI 02005.001](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0502005001). It matches 4 of 5. The one that differs is a $100 office-supply certificate that cannot be resold, which SSA does not count; Giftwise enters every gift card as cash, so the engine counts it and would send the giver to the supplies instead. Two expected answers were corrected after the first run (1 of 5), each recorded with its reason in [`content/eval/held-out.json`](../content/eval/held-out.json); `scripts/run-held-out.ts` reruns it, and `tests/held-out.test.ts` checks the current engine still gives the same answers.

**A second held-out set, written before it ran.** Five more worked examples from SSA's manual, each with SSA's answer and the way the gifts are entered, were written and fixed first in [`content/eval/held-out-2.json`](../content/eval/held-out-2.json), then run once on the engine frozen at commit `c5e94eb`. The results file records the set's git blob hash (`715607f`) and SHA-256, and `tests/held-out.test.ts` fails if the set changes. It matches 1 of 5: a sibling buying her mobile home outright. The four that differ showed three housing rules, now in the engine, each citing its section in the rules table: a home given as itself counts at the presumed maximum value in the month it is given (R-SHEL-03, [POMS SI 00835.400](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500835400) B.2.c and F, SI 00835.310 B, SI 00815.550 C.2), a co-owner's payment of past-due months counts in each month it covers (R-SHEL-04, [POMS SI 00835.360](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500835360) D.3), and rent below market from a parent or child counts as the lesser of the market rent and that value, less the rent paid (R-SHEL-05, [POMS SI 00835.380](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500835380) D, 89 FR 25507).

**After the fix, not a held-out result.** The three rules were written after seeing these five, so this shows only that the current engine follows them; `held-out-2-results.json` stays the frozen run. On the same five as written, the current engine matches 3 of 5. The other two turn on a fact the engine had no field for when the set was written (the payer co-owns the home, `payerCoOwns`; the rent she pays, `rentPaid`); with that fact from each case's own situation in the new field, 5 of 5 (`heldOut2AfterFix()` in `src/lib/held-out.ts`, held by `tests/held-out.test.ts`).

**A third held-out set, written before it ran.** Five more worked examples, from sections of SSA's manual neither earlier set used, each with SSA's answer and the way the gifts are entered, were written and fixed first in [`content/eval/held-out-3.json`](../content/eval/held-out-3.json), then run once on the engine frozen at commit `7e24ed8`. The results file records the set's git blob hash (`b9a5ece`) and SHA-256, and `tests/held-out.test.ts` fails if the set changes. It matches 1 of 5 on every value and 5 of 5 on whether the gift counts: a couple's birthday gift and Bingo winnings, both excluded as infrequent. The four that differ showed three rules, now in the engine, each citing its section in the rules table: a housing bill paid by the week counts as a monthly amount, the weekly amount times 13 divided by 3, a two-week amount halved first (R-SHEL-06, [POMS SI 00835.474](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500835474) B.3); housing help for the whole household is divided equally among the people who live there (R-SHEL-07, [POMS SI 00835.350](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500835350) B.4 and C, SI 00835.474 B.2); and the part of an ABLE account over $100,000 counts toward savings, so a month that starts over the $2,000 limit ($3,000 for a couple) pays no SSI, with Medicaid continuing when the ABLE balance is the reason (R-ABLE-03 with R-RES-01, [POMS SI 01130.740](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0501130740) D.1, [SI 01110.003](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0501110003)). The gift paths use the last one: an ABLE gift that would take the account past $100,000 says what that does to the check, and the router fills the account only to $100,000.

**After the fix on the third set, not a held-out result.** The three rules were written after seeing these five, so this shows only that the current engine follows them; `held-out-3-results.json` stays the frozen run. On the third set's five as written, the current engine matches 3 of 5: the two ABLE examples state their balances in fields the engine already had. The other two turn on a fact the engine had no field for when the set was written (the rent is paid by the week, `every`; she lives with her sister, `householdSize`); with that fact from each case's own situation in the new field, 5 of 5 (`heldOut3AfterFix()` in `src/lib/held-out.ts`, held by `tests/held-out.test.ts`).

**A fourth held-out set, written before it ran.** Fifteen worked examples, from sections of SSA's manual no earlier set used, each with SSA's answer and the way it is entered, were written and fixed first in [`content/eval/held-out-4.json`](../content/eval/held-out-4.json): loans of food and shelter ([POMS SI 00835.482](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500835482)), sharing a household's costs ([SI 00835.160](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500835160)), earmarked sharing ([SI 00835.170](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500835170)), cash from outside the household ([SI 00835.450](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500835450)) and gifts to children with life-threatening conditions ([SI 00830.750](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0500830750)). It ran once on the engine frozen at commit `201128b`; the results file records the set's git blob hash (`49ba930`) and SHA-256. It matches 6 of 15 on every value and 7 of 15 on whether the help counts. The nine that differ showed two rules, now in the engine, each citing its section in the rules table. Living in someone else's household, the person's pro rata share is the household's monthly housing costs divided by the people in it; paying it, or within $20 of it, the household lowers nothing, and paying less, its shelter lowers the check every month, gift or not: by the one-third reduction when it gives her all her meals, and otherwise at her share less what she pays, up to the presumed maximum value (R-SHEL-08, [20 CFR 416.1133](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-K/section-416.1133), 20 CFR 416.1131, SI 00835.160 C.2, SI 00835.340 C.3). Since September 30, 2024 food is not one of those costs, so money she pays for food is not part of her share, and food paid for by others is not counted at all (R-FOOD-01): nothing is counted twice. And a bona fide loan, one she must repay whatever happens with her benefits, that both sides agreed to when it was given, with a plan to repay she can meet, is not income; housing lent from outside counts only for what she need not repay, valued as if there were no loan, and the household's own shelter lent to her counts as what she pays toward her share (R-LOAN-01 and R-LOAN-02, [20 CFR 416.1103(f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-III/part-416/subpart-K/section-416.1103), SI 00835.482 B, E to G, [SI 01120.220](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0501120220)). A loan that fails any test counts like the same help given. Repaying a loan does not lower countable income.

**After the fix on the fourth set, not a held-out result.** The two rules were written after seeing the fifteen, so this shows only that the current engine follows them; `held-out-4-results.json` stays the frozen run. On the fourth set's fifteen as written, the current engine matches 9 of 15: the three arrangements SSA found were not loans (repayment only if SSI starts, no mutual agreement, a plan that is not feasible) now count the household's shelter from the profile alone. With the facts nine cases state in the new fields (the loan, `loan`; the household's costs, `householdCosts`, or the person's share, `proRataShare`; what the person pays, `householdPays`), 15 of 15 (`heldOut4AfterFix()` in `src/lib/held-out.ts`, held by `tests/held-out.test.ts`). Two cases rest on dollar amounts from older years: Alex's plan was not feasible against a $674 check in 2010, and Kathleen's example dates from before the 2024 food rule; the engine takes feasibility and whether the household gives all meals as stated facts, so both score as SSA found.

**How Giftwise treats loans.** The checker, `POST /api/v1/check-gift` and the MCP `check_gift` tool take a loan's facts, so help that already exists can be checked. Giftwise itself moves gifts only: it has no way to record an obligation to repay that meets SSA's tests (an enforceable agreement in effect when the help is given, acknowledged by both sides, unconditional, with a repayment plan she can meet) or to show SSA the borrower's and lender's statements (forms SSA-5062 and SSA-L5063), so it does not offer loans as a route. SSI recipients and families considering one can read POMS SI 00835.482 (food and shelter) and SI 01120.220 (cash).

## Real cases, from federal courts

36 SSI cases from GovInfo's public-domain court opinions where SSA counted help from family or friends (14 scored; the rest turn on loans, shared rent or trusts, listed with the reason). The engine reaches the outcome SSA and the court did in all 14.

- **What routing would have kept, family by family.** In the two cases whose opinions state how long the help lasted, the engine keeps one family $13,584.53 over 41 months and another $24,518.79 over 37 months, $38,103.32 in all, at 2026 rates. Social Security counted help from family or friends against the person in all 14 real SSI court cases scored. Routed through Giftwise, the same help would have kept $331 to $663 a month at 2026 rates in 4 of the 14 as the opinions state the facts, and money in all 10 cases computed in dollars if the person could open an ABLE account: the rules engine's counterfactual on each case's facts (from 1996 to 2014), repriced at 2026 rates, three cases at $331.33 and one at $662.67, $1,656.66 a month across the four. Of the other ten, six were help with housing or everyday living costs, which routing keeps whole only through an ABLE account, and four do not state the amounts the check needs. These are counterfactuals, not outcomes anyone saw.
- **What SSA asked back.** In two cases the opinion states an overpayment: $4,870.00 (a cousin's deposits pushed savings over the limit) and $11,442.20 (children deposited $1,500 to meet a bank's minimum balance). Run through the engine from each case's facts, the same help paid into an ABLE account keeps her savings under $2,000 in every month in both cases, so there would have been nothing to ask back. ABLE needs the disability to have begun before age 46, which these opinions do not state (`overpaymentCheck()` in `scripts/run-field-cases.ts`).
- **What the cases added.** Since 2024 the one-third reduction applies only when the household provides all meals (20 CFR 416.1131(a)(3)); the engine now asks who provides the meals. Where the person lived in a relative's home and paid nothing, the help she got in kind is her share of its costs: routed, the same amount goes to her ABLE account and she pays it to the household as her share (R-SHEL-08; an ABLE withdrawal for housing spent that month is not income, R-ABLE-02), which is how the counterfactual above keeps the reduction off.

- **A second class: help the court held was not income.** The same scorer, run on [`content/eval/field-cases-2.json`](../content/eval/field-cases-2.json) (`npx tsx scripts/run-field-cases.ts --set content/eval/field-cases-2.json`), on three opinions found by searching federal courts for help that did not count: room and board from a son under an agreement to repay ([Ceguerra v. Secretary of HHS, 933 F.2d 735](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F2/933/933.F2d.735.89-16720.html), 9th Cir. 1991), back rent owed to a mother (D. Or. 2019) and about $900 a month lent by an attorney (N.D. Cal. 2024). In a second class, where the court held the help was not income, each case is a loan the person must repay. On its first run, before the engine had a loan field, the engine agreed on 0 of 3. After the loan rules were added (not a first result): 0 of 3 as the cases record the help, and 3 of 3 with the loan each court found in the new loan field. `field-cases-2-results.json` stays the first run; the current engine's line is computed by `courtNotIncomeAfterFix()` in `src/lib/court-cases.ts`, with each court's finding quoted from the case, and shown on [`/evidence/court-cases#not-income`](https://giftwise-405i.onrender.com/evidence/court-cases#not-income).

`npx tsx scripts/run-field-cases.ts` reruns it. Fundraiser pages were not used, because GoFundMe's and Givebutter's terms rule out automated reading.

## Random gifts, every promise kept

`npx tsx scripts/run-invariants.ts` routes 100,000 made-up gifts (seeded, so every run is the same), from one cent to $20,000, up to twelve open needs and people in every living arrangement, and holds each answer to thirteen promises in [`src/lib/invariants.ts`](../src/lib/invariants.ts): every cent placed, her check exactly as with no gift, ABLE within its yearly limit and never past $100,000, at most 10 PayPal recipients, and more. 1,000 of them run with every `npm test`. The first runs sharpened the router: each line is checked together with the lines already chosen, ABLE deposits from one gift share the year's limit, a need marked for ABLE goes only to someone who can have an account, and the month's income is summed to the cent.

## The rules watch the Federal Register

[`/api/rules-watch`](https://giftwise-405i.onrender.com/api/rules-watch) asks the Federal Register, live, for every rule since 2022 that changes how SSI counts income or in-kind support and every notice of the year's amounts, and matches each to what Giftwise does with it ([`content/rules-watch.json`](../content/rules-watch.json)). The nightly outside checks fail on any document not yet reviewed. SSA published the 2027 amounts on October 26, 2026 ([91 FR 67380](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/26/2026-21796/cost-of-living-increase-and-other-determinations-for-2027)), and Giftwise already uses them: November and December 2026 income set the January and February 2027 checks at $1,029, with the one-third reduction and presumed maximum value following the new rate ([POMS SI 02005.001 E.4.c](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0502005001)).

## Checked every night, from outside, on Render Workflows

`workflows/` is a Render Workflow that fans out one task per check, in parallel with retries: each failure case against the PayPal sandbox, each page on the five-minute path, and each public API promise, including the Federal Register watch and a gift paid with a sandbox test card across its payees, with no PayPal login, each capture checked at PayPal (25 in all). A Render cron job starts it nightly at 09:00 UTC; **Run the checks now** on `/evidence/operations` starts it on demand, and the results show there and on `/status`.

- **A race it found in the ledger.** Running the checks in parallel surfaced a race in the audit ledger (two appends at once could fork the hash chain); appends now take a Postgres advisory lock, and `npm run test:ledger-race` fires 60 at once to prove it.
- **A race it found in captures.** Two captures of one order at the same moment get a 500 from PayPal's sandbox on the second request, and the old code took that as final. Captures now retry a 5xx with the same `PayPal-Request-Id`, only the call that records a capture counts it toward the need, and a failure never overwrites a capture.
- **Eight failure cases, run live** on `/evidence/operations`: declined card, double click, unapproved payment, a browser changing the amount or payee, a gift that would lower her check, an AI reviewer that gets the rule wrong, an edited ledger entry, and refunds.
